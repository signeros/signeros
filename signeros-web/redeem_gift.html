THE WORDS ARE THE MONEY — THIS PAGE NEVER ASKS FOR THEM
01Start here

Read this before you touch anything

A gift card is a wallet that was created by somebody else. They wrote the words on the card by hand, which means the words passed in front of their eyes — and possibly in front of a printer, a table, a room, a camera nobody thought about. The card is yours, but the secret behind it was never only yours.

That is the whole reason this page exists. A wallet is only as private as the number of people who could open it, and this one has always had at least two: you, and whoever filled the card in. A secret two people know is not a secret. They almost certainly mean you no harm — that is not the point. The point is that you cannot tell, and you never will be able to, so a gift card is a way to receive bitcoin and not a way to hold it.

So doing nothing is the one answer with a real cost. Everything else on this page ends the same way: the coins are somewhere the card can no longer reach — your bank account, or a wallet whose words only you have ever seen — and the card becomes a keepsake you can show to anybody.

THE FOUR RULES

1. Treat the card like cash. Anyone holding it can take the money. Keep it closed and out of sight until you redeem it.

2. Type the words only into a wallet app you installed yourself — from the official app store listing or the wallet's own site — or into an offline signer. Never into a page that a link, an email or a message brought you to.

3. Nobody legitimate will ever ask for the words. Not support, not an exchange, not the person who gave you the card, not this page. There is no input box anywhere on this site, and there never will be.

4. Do not photograph or upload the card. A photo in a phone gallery is a copy in somebody else's cloud.

// If the amount on the card is small, it is still worth doing this properly — but see what to do about fees first: moving a very small amount can cost more than it holds, and then leaving it on the card until it is worth moving is the sensible answer rather than the lazy one.

02One decision

Spend it, or keep it?

Answer this before you touch the card, because the two answers need different things from you. It is not really a question about bitcoin — it is a question about what you want the money to be.

Spend it

A phone, then an exchange · mostly waiting

You would like this as ordinary money: in your bank account, or spent on something. Bitcoin is a step on the way, not the destination.

  • Open the card's wallet on your phone
  • Send the coins to an exchange in your country
  • Sell them for your own currency, withdraw to your bank
  • Nothing of your own left to look after afterwards
Show me this →

Keep it

Your own wallet first · 20 minutes, or an evening

You would like to hold bitcoin. Then the first job is not the card at all: it is a wallet of your own, because the card is not one.

  • Make a new wallet — on a phone, or offline with SignerOS
  • Write its words on paper, seen by nobody but you
  • Move the gift out of the card and into it
  • Then the card is empty and the secret does not matter
Show me this →
IF YOU CANNOT DECIDE

Pick keep it. It settles the urgent half — the coins stop being reachable by anybody but you — and decides nothing permanent: selling later, from your own wallet, works exactly the same way, and by then the card is out of the story.

What you should not pick is later. The card is the one arrangement where waiting has a cost.

03Spend it

Into money you can spend

Three things happen in order: you open the card's wallet on your phone, you send the coins to an exchange, and the exchange turns them into your local currency and sends that to your bank. Nothing here needs a wallet of your own — the card is emptied in one move, which is what makes this route acceptable despite the words having been seen.

The example uses BlueWallet, because it is free, well known and asks for nothing but the words. Sparrow on a desktop, Nunchuk, Blockstream Green or Electrum do the same job and the steps have the same shape. Install it from the official app store listing or the project's own site and check the name letter by letter — fake wallets exist, and they exist for exactly this moment.

01
ON YOUR PHONE

Install the wallet, then turn off sharing

Open it once and decline everything optional: no cloud backup, no screenshots, no “save to photos”. You are about to type a secret into this app, and you do not want a copy of it leaving the phone.

02
ON YOUR PHONE

Open the card as a wallet

BlueWallet → Add wallet → Import wallet → type the words → Import

Type the words in the order they are numbered on the card, all lower case, single spaces. If the card names a passphrase, put it in the field the app offers for it — without it the same words open a different, empty wallet.

The app checks every word against the standard 2048-word list and refuses a mis-spelling, which is exactly what you want: a wallet that opens at all is a wallet you read correctly.

03
ON YOUR PHONE

Check the balance against the card

The amount should appear within a few seconds. If it says zero, do not re-type anything yet and do not panic — go to Trouble: nine times out of ten it is an address-type setting, not a lost coin. If the card carries a fingerprint, this is where it should match what the app shows.

04
AT THE EXCHANGE

Open an account and finish the identity checks

Pick an exchange that operates legally where you live, that people around you have actually used, and that can pay out to your bank. This page names none: every country has different ones and different rules. Expect an ID document and a selfie, and get all of that done before you send any coins, not after.

05
AT THE EXCHANGE

Get the deposit address — and choose Bitcoin SegWit

Exchange → Deposit → Bitcoin / BTC → network: Bitcoin → format: SegWit

This is the one screen on the whole route where a wrong click loses the money, so it has a box of its own below. Copy the address by paste or QR only — never re-type a bitcoin address — and check the minimum deposit while you are there: many exchanges silently swallow anything below a threshold.

06
ON YOUR PHONE

Send the whole gift

Gift wallet → Send → the deposit address → use full balance → confirm

Choose send max / use full balance so nothing is left behind, and compare the first and last four characters of the address after pasting. A normal fee is fine; there is no hurry. Then wait for the exchange to credit it — it decides how many confirmations, usually one to three blocks, so minutes to an hour.

07
AT THE EXCHANGE

Sell, withdraw, and keep the numbers

Sell the bitcoin for your currency, then withdraw to your bank account. Expect a trading fee, possibly a withdrawal fee, and a bank transfer that may take a day. Do not leave the money sitting on the exchange: an exchange is not a wallet, and what is on one is held by them, not by you.

Write down what you received, what you sold it for, and when. Many countries tax the gain between the two, and the exchange's own statement is what you will be asked for.

08
AFTERWARDS

Retire the card

Delete the imported gift wallet from the app if you like — it holds nothing now. Keep the card as a keepsake or destroy it; both are safe once the transaction has confirmed.

THE ONE SETTING TO GET RIGHT: BITCOIN SEGWIT

An exchange's deposit screen asks two questions that look like the same question. The first is which coin and which network. The answer is Bitcoin's own network, and nothing else on the list is it:

✓ Bitcoin · BTC · on-chain ✗ Lightning ✗ BEP-20 / BSC ✗ ERC-20 / wrapped BTC ✗ Any other chain

Those others are separate rails that happen to have “BTC” in the name. An ordinary send to one of them does not bounce — it is simply gone, and no support desk can reverse it.

The second question is which address format, and the answer there is SegWit — often written Native SegWit, bech32, or shown as an address beginning bc1q. That is the format the gift wallet itself is built around, and it is the cheapest to send to. If the exchange offers Legacy (1…) or SegWit-compatible (3…) instead, they do work — you just pay more fee for the privilege. If it offers no choice at all, the address it gives you is the right one.

Read the address out of the app you are sending from, too. If your wallet shows a warning that the destination is on another network, stop and re-read this box rather than tapping through it.

THREE THINGS THAT SURPRISE PEOPLE

Fees are per transaction, not per amount. Moving a small gift can cost a noticeable slice of it when the network is busy. Check the fee the app proposes before confirming, and if it is absurd, wait a day — fees fall.

The price moves. What the card says was put in is what it was worth then. It may be worth more or less today; neither is a mistake, and neither is anybody's fault.

The identity checks take longer than the bitcoin part. If they drag on for days, the card is sitting there with its secret known the whole time — in that case do keep it first, into a wallet of your own, and sell from there when the account is ready.

04Keep it

Into a wallet that is only yours

Keeping bitcoin on the gift card is the one thing this page argues against, and it has nothing to do with the card being paper. It is that the words were written by a hand that was not yours. So the order matters: your own wallet has to exist before the gift moves anywhere. Make it first, empty the card into it second.

Step one — make a wallet of your own

Two ways, and they differ in one thing: whether your new words are ever typed into a device that can go online. Both give you a real wallet that nobody else has ever seen the words of.

On your phone

≈ 5 minutes · free · good enough for a gift

A self-custody wallet app — BlueWallet, Nunchuk, Blockstream Green, Electrum on a desktop — creates the wallet on the device and shows you its words once. Write them on paper straight away; that paper is the wallet.

  • Add wallet → Bitcoin → SegWit, then create, not import
  • Say no to cloud backup, screenshots and “save to photos”
  • Nobody has ever seen these words — that is the whole difference from the card
  • The words exist on a phone, so the phone is now part of your security
Print a recovery sheet →

Offline, with SignerOS

≈ an evening · a spare computer · a USB stick

SignerOS is a free system on a USB stick that turns a computer you already own into an offline wallet machine. It has no network stack at all, never touches the machine's hard drives, and forgets everything when you switch it off.

  • Create a wallet makes the words in memory and shows them to you
  • You copy them onto paper and type them all back to prove you did
  • The only file it writes is a watch-only one: public keys, safe to carry
  • Your new words never touch anything that could be online

Step two — empty the card into it

This is where the card's words are typed in, on the phone, and it is a one-way trip: the wallet you are about to open is temporary and worth nothing afterwards.

01
YOUR OWN WALLET

Copy one of your own receive addresses

In the wallet you just made, tap Receive and copy the address it shows — it begins bc1q. Keep it on the clipboard, or better, in front of you on the SignerOS screen or a piece of paper. This is where the gift is going.

02
ON YOUR PHONE

Open the card as a second, temporary wallet

BlueWallet → Add wallet → Import wallet → type the card's words → Import

Same as the spend route: the words in their numbered order, all lower case, plus the passphrase if the card names one. The gift's balance appears. If the card carries a fingerprint, check it here.

Give this wallet a name like gift — temporary if the app lets you. It is not your wallet and it is not meant to survive the evening.

03
ON YOUR PHONE

Send everything to your own address

Gift wallet → Send → your own bc1q address → use full balance → confirm

Choose send max / use full balance so nothing is left behind, and compare the first and last four characters of the address after pasting. Both wallets are on the Bitcoin network, so there is no network to choose here — only the address, and it must be one your own wallet showed you.

04
YOUR OWN WALLET

Watch it arrive, then throw the card's wallet away

The amount appears in your own wallet within minutes, occasionally an hour. Once it has one confirmation, delete the temporary gift wallet from the app: its words are worthless now, and that is the point. The money is behind words that nobody has ever seen but you.

05
AFTERWARDS

Look after the paper, not the app

The app can be deleted, the phone can be lost or replaced — the words on your paper are what restores the wallet. Keep that paper the way you would keep the same amount in cash: somewhere dry, somewhere private, and not photographed. There is a printable sheet for it that fetches nothing and can be printed from the offline machine.

WHY THIS IS FINISHED, AND THE CARD ROUTE NEVER WAS

The gift card's words were seen by somebody else and cannot be un-seen, so the money behind them was never entirely yours. The words you wrote in step one were shown once, on a screen you were holding, and written by your own hand. That is the difference the whole exercise buys, and it lasts as long as the paper does.

05Fully offline

Keeping it without the words going online at all

This is keep it done to the end. In section 04 your own words never touch a network, but the card's words are typed into a phone — and for a gift-sized amount, moved the same evening, that is a reasonable trade. Here even that is avoided: the card's words go into a machine that cannot go online, and only a finished, signed transaction ever crosses over on a USB stick.

SignerOS is the free system that makes the machine. It has no network stack at all, it never touches the computer's hard drives, and it forgets everything when you switch it off. What it is and how to get it → You will also need one wallet program on your everyday computer to watch balances and build the transaction — Sparrow in the steps below — which never sees a secret.

Two things happen at once on this route: you create a wallet of your own, whose words are written on paper and have never been anywhere near a network, and you empty the gift card into it with the card's words never reaching an online machine either.

01
OFFLINE MACHINE

Boot SignerOS and create your own wallet

Home → Create a wallet → write the words on paper → type them all back → name the file

Write those words on paper and keep no other copy — that paper is the wallet. There is a printable sheet for it: the recovery sheet. SignerOS then writes one watch-only file of public keys to the stick's PSBT_DATA drive. It cannot spend anything, so it is safe to carry.

02
EVERYDAY COMPUTER

Load that file into a coordinator

Open the watch-only file in Sparrow (or any wallet that imports output descriptors). This is now the destination: it can show balances and build transactions, and it holds nothing secret. Copy one of its receive addresses and keep it to hand.

03
OFFLINE MACHINE

Describe the gift wallet, offline

Home → Export watch-only keys → type the card's words → review → name the file

Type the card's words — and its passphrase, if it has one — into the offline machine. It derives the same public keys the giver's wallet has and writes a second watch-only file. The words are wiped from memory when you leave the screen, and nothing derived from them is written anywhere.

The review page shows a fingerprint and the first address. If the card carries a fingerprint, this is where the two must match.

04
EVERYDAY COMPUTER

Import it as a temporary wallet and build the sweep

Load the second file into Sparrow as well. The gift balance appears. Select all of its coins, send them to the address from step 02 with send max, and save the transaction as a .psbt file onto the PSBT_DATA drive instead of signing it — there is nothing here that could sign it.

05
OFFLINE MACHINE

Sign it, with your own eyes on the numbers

Home → Sign a transaction → pick the .psbt → read amount and address → type the card's words → sign

SignerOS shows what the transaction actually pays and to whom, refuses anything whose fee it cannot determine, and only then asks for the words. Check that the destination is your own address and that the amount is the whole gift. Sign, and the signed file is written back to the stick.

06
EVERYDAY COMPUTER

Broadcast, then throw the gift wallet away

Open the signed file in Sparrow and broadcast it. Once it confirms, delete the temporary gift wallet: its words are now worthless, and the money sits behind the words on your own paper.

WHY TYPING THE CARD'S WORDS TWICE IS STILL SAFE HERE

Both times they go into a machine that has no network stack compiled into it, no drivers for your disks, and no storage of its own — it runs from RAM and loses everything at power-off. The only thing that ever leaves it is a file you named, and that file contains public keys or a signed transaction.

06Trouble

When it does not look right

What you seeWhat it usually means
The balance is zero, but the card says there should be something Most often the app is looking at the wrong address type. A SignerOS gift is normally funded on the addresses that begin bc1q (Native SegWit, m/84'/0'/0'). Re-import and choose that type, or let the app scan all of them. Then check the fingerprint: if it does not match the card, a word was read wrong — most often a/o, rn/m, or a missing final letter.
The app rejects the words One of them is not in the standard list, or the last word does not match the checksum built into the rest. Re-read your handwriting: the list has no proper nouns, no capitals and no words shorter than three letters. Every word is unique in its first four letters, so those four decide it.
The card mentions a passphrase Then it is part of the secret: enter it exactly, including capitals and spaces. The words without it open a valid, empty wallet — which looks exactly like a wrongly-copied word. If the passphrase is lost, the money is lost; ask the giver while they still remember.
The exchange asks which network to deposit on Answer Bitcoin — the coin's own network, sometimes written BTC or on-chain. If it then asks for an address format, take SegWit / bech32, the address beginning bc1q. Lightning, BEP-20, ERC-20 and “wrapped” BTC are different rails: an ordinary send to one of them is not bounced back, it is lost. There is a box about exactly this screen.
You are not sure the wallet you made is really yours The test is one question: did it show you a list of words and ask you to write them down? If yes, it is self-custody and the words are the wallet. If it only asked for an email and a password, it is an account at a company — moving the gift there swaps one stranger for another and adds a login you can be locked out of.
The coins left the gift wallet but have not arrived Look at the receiving side, not the sending side. An exchange credits a deposit only after the confirmations it requires, and only if the deposit is above its minimum. Your own wallet shows an incoming payment straight away, usually as “pending”. If both show nothing after an hour, check the address you actually sent to — the first and last four characters against the one you copied.
The fee is a large share of the gift Fees depend on how busy the network is, not on the amount. Wait for a quieter day — weekends are often cheaper — or leave it where it is until you actually need it. If the amount is truly tiny, sweeping it may never be economic; treat the card as the keepsake it is.
The card is years old Bitcoin does not expire and neither do the words. An old card is fine; the amount is simply worth whatever it is worth today.
You think somebody else has seen the words Move the money now, by whichever route you can do fastest. Speed beats elegance: a sweep into a phone wallet you make in two minutes is better than a perfect offline setup next weekend.
The card is lost, or was thrown away with the wrapping Then the wallet cannot be opened by anyone, including you. There is no reset, no support line and no recovery — this is the part of bitcoin that has no customer service. Search the bin.
NEVER TYPE THE WORDS INTO A WEB PAGE

No legitimate site has a box for them — including this one. A page that asks is stealing, however official it looks.

NEVER SEND THEM IN A MESSAGE

Not to the giver, not to “support”, not to a friend who offers to help. Help can be given without the words.

NEVER PHOTOGRAPH THE CARD

The photo syncs to a cloud, the cloud has an account, the account has a password. You have just made your bitcoin as strong as that password.

NEVER LET SOMEONE “HELP” BY SCREEN SHARE

The classic theft of gift-card bitcoin is a helpful stranger watching the words being typed in.

07Giving one

If you are the one making the gift

A gift card is only a real gift if the words end up in exactly one place: on the card. That is easy to get wrong, so here is the whole discipline in seven lines. There is a printable card to fill in: the gift card sheet — pick the occasion, type the name and the note, print it blank and write the words by hand.

  1. Create the wallet on an offline machine. SignerOS's Create a wallet does it in RAM and writes nothing private, ever. Twelve words is plenty for a gift and much less to copy.
  2. Write the words on the card only. Not in a note app, not in a photo, not in a password manager “just in case”. If you keep a copy, you have given a wallet you can still empty.
  3. Use no passphrase unless you have a reason. It is one more thing the receiver can lose, and it protects nothing that the sealed card does not already protect.
  4. Prove the wallet before you fund it. The machine writes a watch-only file of public keys to the stick; open it in a coordinator — Sparrow, Blockstream Green — and check that the master fingerprint and the first address it shows are the ones the machine showed you. That is the whole check, and it is what proves the address you are about to fund belongs to the words on the card. Every screen of it →
  5. Fund the first receive address — the one beginning bc1q — and write the fingerprint, the amount and the date on the card so the receiver can check they opened the right wallet.
  6. Send a test amount first if the gift is significant, and confirm it arrives before sending the rest.
  7. Hand it over closed, and say the one sentence that matters: move it into your own wallet soon, and never type these words into a web page.
  8. Point them at this page. The card carries the address and a QR of it, which is the whole reason it exists.

// Doing it the other way round — buying on an exchange and sending to an address the receiver gives you — is also perfectly good, and needs no card at all. The card is for when you want the gift to be a physical thing that works even if you are not around to explain it.
