Bitcoin, in plain words

Simply.
Bitcoin

Bitcoin is not a file, and it is not inside your wallet. It is a line in a list that tens of thousands of computers each keep a copy of — and owning it means knowing the one secret that can move that line. Almost everything that feels strange about Bitcoin follows from those two sentences.

01Where it is

What Bitcoin is, and where it actually lives

Bitcoin is a ledger: a public list of every transaction ever made, kept in identical copies on tens of thousands of computers around the world. Anybody can download the whole list, and they all agree on what it says.

A bitcoin is not an object in that list. It is an entry — a line saying that a certain amount can be spent by whoever can produce one particular signature. That is the entire substance of it. There is no coin, no file, no folder of money sitting on a disk somewhere waiting to be copied off.

So when somebody asks where their bitcoin is, the honest answer is: in the ledger, everywhere the ledger is copied, and in no single place you could point at. If every device you own burned tonight, the ledger would not change by a single character — because your coins were never on those devices to begin with.

02What owning means

Two kinds of property: one physical, one informational

The clearest way to understand Bitcoin ownership is to put it next to gold, because they are opposites in exactly one respect — and that one respect explains everything else.

Gold

Physical property

You own gold by holding it. It is in your hand, your safe, or a vault with your name on the paperwork. Whoever physically has the metal can sell the metal.

Ownership is possession, and possession is a fact about the physical world. To take it from you, somebody has to reach the metal — and once they have, you no longer have it. You would notice.

Bitcoin

Informational property

You own bitcoin by knowing. The ledger will move those coins for anyone who can produce the right signature, and exactly one thing produces it: a secret number called the private key.

Ownership is knowledge. There is no object to hold and nothing to reach for — which is a strength and a danger at once, and it is worth being precise about both.

▲ THE PART THAT CUTS BOTH WAYS

Information can be copied. A thief does not have to take your key away from you — they only have to learn it, and you would still have your words, and the coins would still be gone. That is why a recovery phrase must never be typed into an online computer, photographed, or stored in a cloud. And it runs the other way too: nobody can restore what you forget. There is no support line and no password reset, because there is no company holding your coins — only the ledger, and the key.

03Not in your device

Your bitcoin is not in your hardware wallet

It is not on your computer either, and not on the USB stick in your drawer. None of those hold coins. What they hold is the key — or the twelve or twenty-four recovery words the key is derived from. A wallet is a keyholder, not a vault.

Which means the form your words happen to take does not matter to the ledger at all. Written on paper, stamped into a metal plate, sitting inside a signing device, or held only in your memory: the ledger cannot tell the difference and does not try. Same key, same signature, same coins.

your recovery words → a private key → a signature → the ledger accepts the spend

Two things follow from that, and they tend to surprise people in opposite directions.

✓ BREAK THE DEVICE, LOSE NOTHING

The coins were never in it. Get another wallet, enter the same words, and you are exactly where you were. A drowned, burnt or stolen device is an inconvenience, not a loss — provided the words exist somewhere else.

✕ KEEP THE DEVICE, LOSE EVERYTHING

If the words are gone and the device fails, so are the coins. They stay visible on the ledger forever, belonging to a key nobody can produce any more. The backup is the asset, and the hardware is a convenience.

■ WHAT "MOVING YOUR BITCOIN" REALLY MEANS

Nothing is transported anywhere. Either you move the key — carry the same words to a different wallet, and the coins do not move at all, because they never belonged to the old device — or you make a new ledger entry saying the amount now answers to a different key. Only the second one is a transaction, and only the second one costs a fee.

04What follows

If ownership is knowledge, there are only two ways to lose it

Somebody else learns your key, or you lose it. Every piece of Bitcoin security advice you will ever read is aimed at one of those two — and the two pull against each other, because every extra copy you make so you cannot lose it is one more place somebody could find it. Getting the balance right for the amount you hold is the whole job.

It is also what a signing device is for. The key is generated and kept on a machine that never goes online, and what leaves that machine is a signature — never the key itself. The ledger only ever needed the signature.

That is what this project builds: SignerOS turns a computer you already own into an offline signing machine, booted from a USB stick, with no network support compiled into it at all. 05Straight answers

Questions people actually ask

Where are my bitcoins actually stored?

On the blockchain — a public ledger kept in identical copies on tens of thousands of computers. Your coins are an entry saying an amount can be spent by whoever produces a particular signature. They are not a file, and they are not on any device you own.

Is my bitcoin inside my hardware wallet?

No. A hardware wallet holds a key, not coins. It is a keyholder, not a vault, and the coins stay on the ledger whatever happens to the device.

What happens to my bitcoin if my hardware wallet breaks or is stolen?

Nothing happens to the coins. With your recovery words you enter them into another wallet and you are where you were. A thief who has the device but not your PIN or passphrase still cannot sign. What you must never lose is the words.

Can someone steal my bitcoin without touching any of my devices?

Yes — if they learn your key or your recovery words. Information can be copied, so the theft leaves everything of yours where it was: you would still have your words, and the coins would still be gone. That is why the words never go into an online machine, a photograph or a cloud.

Does it matter whether my phrase is on paper, on metal, or only in my head?

Not to the ledger — it cannot tell the difference. It matters enormously to you: paper burns, metal survives a fire, and memory fails silently, without warning and without a way to check. Memorising a phrase and keeping no physical backup is one of the reliable ways people lose coins permanently.

What happens if I lose my recovery words?

The coins stay on the ledger and nobody can move them, including you. There is no support line, no password reset and no authority to appeal to. Forgetting is a way of losing property that has no real equivalent in the physical world.
